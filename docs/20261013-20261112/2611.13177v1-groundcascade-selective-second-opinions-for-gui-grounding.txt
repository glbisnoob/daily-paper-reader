## Abstract 

Graphical user interface (GUI) grounding maps a screen-shot and a natural-language instruction to the screen lo-cation where an agent should act. Zoomed views can re-veal small targets but may lose the context needed to iden-tify them. We introduce GroundCascade, a training-free controller that uses agreement between a grounder’s full-screen and zoomed predictions to decide when to consult a complementary model. It selects between zoomed predic-tions while preserving corroborated full-screen locations. An optional Finalizer predicts from an unmarked joint crop when disagreement remains (Full); omitting it yields the two-stage Selective variant. GroundCascade reaches 85.33% on ScreenSpot-Pro with Indeed-UI-32B/8B/32B and 88.43% on the visible-target OSWorld-G-Refined sub-set with Qwen3.6-27B/3.8-27B/3.8-27B, gains of 2.66 and 2.35 percentage points over the stronger standalone two-pass grounder. On UI-Vision, GroundCascade reaches 72.20% category-balanced accuracy, with Selective slightly higher at 72.28%. Across these three evaluations, GroundCascade in Full mode uses 2.42–2.73 calls per re-quest, 31–39% fewer than unconditionally acquiring both models’ two-pass predictions. At matched second-model call budgets, selective acquisition gains 1.52–2.56 points over standalone Two-pass, compared with 0.22–0.69 points from stratified random acquisition. GroundCascade leads the official ScreenSpot-Pro leaderboard and exceeds re-ported GUI-specialist comparisons on OSWorld-G-Refined and UI-Vision, subject to differences in external evaluation protocols.

## 2. Related Work 

Zoom and regional refinement. Iterative zooming and region search increase the effective resolution available for small targets [8, 19, 20]; region ranking and visual-token processing refine how crops are used [3, 17]. BAMI and FastFocus combine coarse-to-fine views with candidate se-lection or verification [37, 40], and GUI-Lens chooses crops for a general-purpose VLM [5]. Zooming can also discard needed context [8]. UI-Zoomer decides when to zoom from sampled consensus and token confidence [27]. MVP aggre-gates attention-guided views [42]; we evaluate its released pipeline with the same checkpoint. AutoFocus separates prediction, verification, and aggregation during active vi-sual search [38]; Chain-of-Ground, GMS, and MHVF like-wise use feedback, regional specialization, or hypothesis verification [2, 13, 16]. 

Confidence, agreement, and verification. Zoom Consis-tency [10] uses consistency to route between two acquired two-pass pipelines and discusses early stopping in further zoom steps. GroundCascade instead uses the first pair to de-cide whether to acquire the second, with a separate, tighter test for preserving full-screen predictions. The matched unconditional-acquisition control is not a reproduction of Zoom Consistency’s router. SafeGround calibrates sampled spatial dispersion for deferral [31]; PACE uses coordinate-token entropy [15], and ARGUS benchmarks grounding un-certainty [12]. Unlike learned reflection [33, 41], Ground-Cascade’s Finalizer makes an ordinary grounding predic-tion with the original instruction. 2Model cascades. Cascades allocate computation selec-tively in detection [30], neural networks [34], and lan-guage models [1, 39]. Deferral theory distinguishes confi-dence in the current predictor from its performance relative to a downstream model [9]. Adaptive VLM Routing and LEASH study escalation in computer-use settings [18, 32]. GroundCascade routes using only two-pass coordinates, without token probabilities or hidden states. 

## 3. GroundCascade

q be an instruction. A grounding model M maps (I, q ) to a point or an invalid output. Valid points use zero-based screenshot coordinates, 0 ≤ x < W and 0 ≤ y < H . We use three views. The Context view is the full screenshot, giving pM,C = M (I, q ). The Detail view is a crop BM of nominally half the screen width and height, centered on the Context point and clipped to the screenshot. Its prediction

4.2. Main results 

Table 1 compares the three reported allocations with the stronger standalone Context and Two-pass models on each benchmark. On SSP, GroundCascade (I32/I8/I32) reaches 85.33% (1,349/1,581), compared with 82.67% for stan-dalone Two-pass: 42 additional correct answers, or +2 .66 

points (paired 95% CI [ +1.58, +3.80] points). Selec-tive reaches 84.19%; GroundCascade uses 2.56 calls/query, 36.11% fewer than unconditional acquisition. On OSG-R, GroundCascade (Q36/Q38/Q38) reaches 88.43% (451/510), compared with 86.08% for standalone Q36 Two-pass: 12 additional correct answers, or +2 .35 

points (image-clustered 95% CI [+0.95, +3.86] points). Selective reaches 88.04%; GroundCascade uses 2.42 calls/query, saving 39.28%. On UIV, GroundCascade (Q36/Q38/Q38) reaches 72.20% category-balanced accuracy (3951/5479 correct, 72.11% pooled), a +2 .48 -point gain over standalone Q36 Two-pass (69.72%; image-clustered 95% CI [+1.69, +3.28] points). Omitting the Finalizer yields 72.28% in Selective mode, a +2 .56 -point gain over the same baseline (95% CI [+1.82, +3.30] points). Q38 alone scores 64.07% in Two-pass, but complements Q36.

## 5. Error Detection and Recovery 

We analyze routing, recovery, and remaining errors for the same three allocations reported in Table 1. 

5.1. Agreement as a routing signal 

Smaller Context-to-Detail displacement predicts greater re-liability: using negative displacement to rank Grounder De-tail correctness gives AUROC 0.718 on SSP. At the de-ployed threshold, the first gate escalates 157 of 274 stan-dalone Two-pass errors on SSP and 42 of 71 on Qwen OSG-R. Supplementary Tab. S4 reports precision and re-call across all three benchmarks. Escalation concentrates errors but also subjects some correct predictions to further inference. 

5.2. Recovery and preservation 

Zooming can turn correct predictions wrong [8, 27]. On SSP, it spoils 42 correct Context predictions; the first gate flags 38, and the cascade restores 28. Selecting only be-tween Detail predictions cannot directly recover a correct full-screen location lost through zooming. Stage 2 can instead preserve the more self-consistent model’s Context prediction when both full-screen predictions agree within 

τC along each axis. On SSP, the Context-agreement branch returns 13 correct predictions on 17 requests, versus 12 from the preferred De-tail candidate. Across these benchmarks, Context preserva-tion contributes less than the Detail-based routing decisions. Detail itself repairs 184 Context errors, of which 165 remain correct after GroundCascade. All 19 cascade regressions relative to standalone Two-pass lose a successful zoom cor-rection. The controller must balance recovery of Context with preservation of useful Detail.

5.3. Where errors remain 

Figure 6 follows SSP requests to their exit stage and marks errors made after a correct prediction had already been ac-quired; supplementary Fig. S1 does the same for OSG-R and UIV. On SSP, 117 of 232 errors exit at the Grounder, and both its predictions are wrong in 113 of them. The target lies entirely inside the Detail crop for 72 stable er-rors and entirely outside it for 43, with two partial overlaps. Stable errors therefore include mistakes within an available crop. On SSP, cross-model displacement better ranks in-correct Grounder Detail predictions than self-displacement (AUROC 0.807 vs. 0.718), but requires an additional Re-grounder Context call. Some incorrect returns occur even though an earlier ac-quired prediction is correct. On SSP, this happens for 26 of 60 errors on the Regrounder’s Detail-agreement route, 1 of 4 on its Context-agreement route, and 17 of 51 at the Fi-nalizer. On Qwen OSG-R, 15 Regrounder-exit errors like-wise had a correct acquired candidate; on UIV, these counts are 277 at R and 173 at F . The Finalizer recovers 6 of 40 SSP requests despite four incorrect earlier predictions; Fig. 3 shows two recoveries, one from this cohort. 

## 6. Conclusion 

GroundCascade uses agreement between two views to de-cide when to acquire a complementary grounder. Its Selec-tive variant preserves corroborated full-screen predictions alongside zoomed candidates; the Full controller adds a fresh joint-view prediction when disagreements remain. On SSP and OSG-R, GroundCascade improves on the stronger standalone two-pass model by 2.66 and 2.35 points; on UIV, Selective improves by 2.56 points, with no further gain from finalization. Across the three Full allocations, the con-6troller directs complementary predictions toward useful cor-rections while reducing model invocations by 31–39% rel-ative to unconditional two-model acquisition.

## S2. Evaluation details 

Scoring. Target boxes include their boundaries. OSG-R consists of 470 rectangles and 40 polygons; polygons use the benchmark’s ray-crossing test. UIV contains 1,772 Basic, 1,772 Functional, and 1,935 Spatial queries across 1,181 screenshot files representing 1,156 distinct images. Its overall score equally averages category accuracies. 

Published OSG-R bounds. The original benchmark has 564 requests, including 510 visible-target cases. If a published one-decimal percentage a is single-run micro-accuracy on all 564, its visible-target accuracy is at most 

(a + 0 .05) · 564 /510 ; Fig. 1 rounds this ceiling upward to 0.1 point. The four reported scores 79.1, 78.5, 78.5, and 76.4% correspond to 446, 443, 443, and 431 correct of 564, respectively, but none can arise by rounding integer correct counts out of 510. This supports, without proving, the 564-item micro-accuracy interpretation: averaging runs or cate-gories could also yield those percentages. Refusal outcomes are unavailable. The starred, hatched ≤ bars are therefore 

conditional bounds , not observed subset scores or matched evaluations. 

MVP interface. Released MVP uses I32 on all 1,581 SSP requests with its own prompt, parser, attention-guided views and four-call interface; its image cap is 4096 × 2160 with 32 output tokens. 

Controls and uncertainty. The standalone Two-pass base-line returns valid Detail, otherwise valid Context; invalid Context skips Detail. Call savings compare Full’s executed invocations with acquiring both models’ Two-pass predic-tions on every request, including the same invalid-Context skip. This unconditional control returns the valid Detail with smaller Context–Detail displacement (ties favor the Grounder), falling back to the other valid Detail; it omits Context return and finalization.
