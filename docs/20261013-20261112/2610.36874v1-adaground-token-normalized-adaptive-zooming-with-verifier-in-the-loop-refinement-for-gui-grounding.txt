Abstract 

GUI grounding on high-resolution screens fails mostly on small targets, and zooming in is a common training-free remedy. However, most existing zooming pipelines do not match the zoom to the target’s size, accept the zoomed answer unconditionally and never revisit a wrong answer. To address these issues, we propose AdaGround, a training-free framework with three components: (1) Token-Normalized Adaptive Zooming , which sets the magnifi-cation, field of view and zoom depth of each sample from the model’s own estimate of the target size; (2) Verifier-in-the-Loop Refine-ment , which uses one marked-click Yes/No verifier to keep or reject each zoom, choose be-tween zoom chains, trigger re-grounding and, with an existence query, decide when to abstain; and (3) Compositional Structure Grounding ,which composes spreadsheet cells from their headers. On Qwen3-VL models, AdaGround surpasses state-of-the-art training-free zoom-ing methods and many trained GUI models, improving accuracy over the backbone by 7.4 to 20.6 points on OSWorld-G and ScreenSpot-Pro, and its gains carry over to held-out UI-Vision. Code is available at https://github. com/bcwang-sjtu/AdaGround .

1 Introduction

Their perceptual core is GUI ground-ing , which takes an instruction and a screenshot and localizes the target element precisely enough to act on it (Zheng et al., 2024; Hui et al., 2025). Shortcomings of training-free refinement. 

Most current refinement pipelines share five shortcomings. (i) Scale-agnostic zoom geometry. 

Iterative narrowing (Nguyen, 2024), Zoom-Click (Jiang et al., 2025), AdaZoom-GUI (Pei et al., 2026), UI-Zoomer (Tang et al., 2026a) and MVP (Zhang et al., 2026d) set the crop by fixed ratios, size triggers, candidate spread or fixed-size attention windows. None relates the zoom to the number of visual tokens the target will occupy in the rendered view. (ii) Open-loop acceptance. The zoomed prediction replaces the coarse one unconditionally, even when the crop has discarded the context that identified the target.

Attention-guided methods (Xu et al., 2025) such as MVP need attention maps from inside the backbone. MVP also aggregates its views by spatial voting, which cannot tell which of two disagreeing views is right. (v) Always zooming and answering. Targets absent from the screen are solved by no view, and targets defined by their position in a structure, like a spreadsheet cell, lose their evidence in a tight zoom. Yet every pipeline zooms and answers. 

AdaGround. Our key observation is that differ-ently zoomed views fail on different samples. On Qwen3-VL-8B ScreenSpot-Pro, a fixed crop and an adaptive one are right on 63 .5% and 59 .6% of samples, but at least one of them is right on 68 .9% .Refinement should therefore not commit to one zoom but choose, per sample, which zoomed an-swer to trust. The grounder itself can make that choice when shown a marked click. We propose 

AdaGround (Figure 2), a training-free framework in which the grounder sizes its own zoom and veri-fies its own answers. Token-Normalized Adaptive Zooming (TNZ) answers (i). From the model’s own bounding box on the coarse view, it solves per sample for the magnification, the crop side and the number of zoom stages. They are chosen so that the target spans a fixed number of visual tokens while the crop still covers the coarse localization error. Verifier-in-the-Loop Refinement (VLR) an-swers (ii)–(iv) and the absent targets of (v). One marked-click Yes/No verifier drives verification-gated acceptance , a verifier-arbitrated chain port-folio and negative-feedback re-grounding . The port-folio selects between the TNZ chain and a fixed-crop chain by verification instead of by vote, and re-grounding runs with the rejected location crossed out. With a companion existence query, the verifier also decides existence-gated abstention . Compo-sitional Structure Grounding (CSG) answers the structural targets of (v) by composing a spreadsheet cell from its separately grounded column and row headers. Throughout, the VLM is a black box that takes images and text and returns text and first-token probabilities. One set of constants serves both backbones and all benchmarks (Section 3.5). 

Results. On OSWorld-G (Xie et al., 2026) and ScreenSpot-Pro, with Qwen3-VL-8B and Qwen3-VL-32B, AdaGround is above every published re-sult of MVP and ZoomClick (Figure 1), by up to 

7.3 points. The lead is significant in every paired test against MVP’s per-sample predictions. Ada-Ground is also above all proprietary models and trained GUI specialists in Tables 1 and 2, including 72B ones. Its gains are largest on ScreenSpot-Pro’s high-resolution screens ( +14 .9 and +20 .6 points 2over the single pass) and carry over to held-out UI-Vision (Nayak et al., 2025). On ScreenSpot-v2 (Wu et al., 2025b), whose large targets the single pass already resolves, they are not significant. Our contributions are as follows: • TNZ sizes every zoom in visual tokens from the model’s own estimate of the target, so that the target reaches a legible span at any screen resolution (Property 1). • VLR makes the grounder its own verifier. One marked-click Yes/No question replaces open-loop acceptance and voting, and it decides re-grounding and abstention. • CSG composes structural targets from separately grounded headers instead of zooming. 

2 Related Work 

GUI grounding. GUI grounding is commonly cast as coordinate generation by a VLM (Cheng et al., 2024; Bai et al., 2025a; Guo et al., 2025), following UI-specialised models (Baechler et al., 2024; You et al., 2024). Specialists fine-tuned on GUI corpora (Gou et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025b; Hong et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2026) and reinforcement post-training or self-distillation (Liu et al., 2026b; Tang et al., 2026b; Zhou et al., 2026; Qiu et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026c) improve the grounder itself. AdaGround instead changes how an off-the-shelf grounder is queried. 

Test-time zooming. Iterative narrowing (Nguyen, 2024) re-predicts on crops of fixed geometry around the previous answer, RegionFocus (Luo et al., 2025) and DiMo-GUI (Wu et al., 2025a) zoom into candidate regions, and V ∗ (Wu and Xie, 2024) searches for small objects outside GUIs. Three recent systems refine the zoom. ZoomClick (Jiang et al., 2025) crops with a fixed shrink ratio for a fixed three iterations. AdaZoom-GUI (Pei et al., 2026) uses a trained grounder’s box as a small-or-large trigger and then mag-nifies by a predefined ratio. UI-Zoomer (Tang et al., 2026a) sizes one crop from the variance of eight sampled candidates, scaled by a model-and benchmark-specific constant. None derives the zoom from the target’s token span, and all three accept the zoomed prediction uncondition-ally (shortcomings (i) and (ii)). Other training-free pipelines progressively zoom onto interface ele-ments (Liu et al., 2026d), refine through multi-step visual reasoning (Li et al., 2025a) or search over screen regions (Liu et al., 2026a). Others miti-gate grounding biases (Zhang et al., 2026a), crop coarse-to-fine with OCR and detected components as cues (Fu et al., 2026), or guide a visual search by the perplexity of coordinate tokens (Yao et al., 2026). 

Attention-guided and vote-based aggregation. 

MVP (Zhang et al., 2026d), the attention-guided zooming method we compare against, extracts attention-cropped views and aggregates their pre-dictions by spatial clustering. Attention-driven grounding (Xu et al., 2025) likewise reads the model’s attention, and GUI-RC (Du et al., 2026) votes over sampled predictions on a spatial grid. Concurrent with our work, Re-Prefill (Lin et al., 2026a) selects visual tokens from the attention of a first prefill and modifies the model’s prefill in a second pass. Like MVP, it needs model inter-nals. AdaGround reads only the generated text and first-token probabilities, and replaces voting with verification (shortcoming (iv)). 

Verification and feedback. GUI-Cursor (Zhao et al., 2026) and UI-Ins (Chen et al., 2026) train spatial feedback and instruction reasoning into the grounder. LookAgain (Zhang et al., 2026b) and Measure Twice, Click Once (Wang et al., 2026b) train the grounder or a visual critic to re-examine its clicks. SafeGround (Wang et al., 2026a) and Kumar et al. (2026) calibrate and benchmark un-certainty in GUI grounding. AdaGround obtains these signals from the unmodified model through one Yes/No question about a marked click and an existence question (shortcomings (iii) and (v)). 

Learned focusing and agent memory. Fo-cusUI (Ouyang et al., 2026) learns a position-preserving selection of visual tokens, GUI-Spotlight (Lei et al., 2026) trains a model to narrow its focus, and DeepEyes (Zheng et al., 2026) and AdaptVision (Lin et al., 2026b) learn to request zooms. Agent frameworks add visual procedural memory (Liu et al., 2026c) or evolve skills from traces (Hu et al., 2026; Xiao et al., 2026). Ada-Ground is training-free and solves whether and how far to zoom from the model’s own size estimate.

3.1 Problem Formulation 

Given a screenshot S ∈ RH×W ×3 and an instruc-tion I, GUI grounding predicts a point p = ( x, y ),which is correct iff p ∈ B, the ground-truth region of the referenced element. When the instruction has no target on screen, the only correct output is an abstention, and an abstention on any other sample is an error. An off-the-shelf VLM M , used with-out any weight update, receives a rendered view and the instruction and emits a coordinate as text, 

p = M (R(S, b ), I ). The renderer R(S, b ) scales the area of S by b, so the linear scale is √b and 

b > 1 up-samples. Access to M is black-box. We read its generated text and first-token probabilities, and never its attention maps, hidden states or gradi-ents. 

Token pitch and token span. Encoders that work at dynamic resolution tile a rendered view into patches and merge neighbouring patches into one visual token. Qwen3-VL (Bai et al., 2025a) merges 2 × 2 patches of 16 pixels, so one token covers a square of side π = 32 rendered pixels. For a target whose shorter side measures s original pixels, shown in a view of linear scale k relative to the screenshot, the token span 

σ = s k π (1) is the number of visual tokens the target occupies along its shorter side in the view the model is given. Because it is measured after rendering, it is com-parable across resolutions, crops and up-sampling. Accuracy climbs steeply below about three tokens and gains much less above (Appendix E). This mo-tivates two design choices of TNZ. Every zoom is normalized to a target span of τ tokens, and the first zoom fires under a looser criterion than later ones.

3.4 Compositional Structure Grounding 

A spreadsheet cell is defined by its place in a struc-ture, not by its appearance. The cell is identified by its column and row headers. These lie out-side any crop tight enough to magnify the cell, so zooming tends to discard the evidence. CSG changes the primitive instead of the view. The same model, reading the instruction text only, rewrites 

I into a program ρ ∈ { GROUND , CELL (col , row )}

(the router of Figure 2, whose ‘element’ branch is GROUND ). Any other output is executed as 

> GROUND

. CELL (col , row ) grounds descriptions of the column header and of the row header sepa-rately. Each header is grounded first on the whole screenshot and then on a crop of side d0/2 around that point at 3× linear magnification, which gives 

(xcol , y col ) and (xrow , y row ). The program then composes 

p = ( xcol , y row ) iff xcol > x row , y col < y row , (9) i.e. when the column header lies to the right of and above the row header, as in a spreadsheet grid. Otherwise it falls back to the VLR answer.

5 Conclusion 

We presented AdaGround , a training-free, black-box framework that addresses five shortcomings of test-time refinement for GUI grounding. With one configuration shared by Qwen3-VL-8B and Qwen3-VL-32B, AdaGround scores above MVP’s published results on OSWorld-G and ScreenSpot-Pro and above many trained GUI models, including 72B ones. It is also significantly above its own fixed 

4× crop chain on ScreenSpot-Pro and on held-out UI-Vision. The lesson for test-time refinement is that zoom geometries fail on different samples and that the grounder itself, asked about a marked click, can choose between them. On ScreenSpot-Pro, this choice recovers more than a third of the headroom above the better chain. 

Limitations 

Scope. We evaluate Qwen3-VL-8B and Qwen3-VL-32B (FP8 weights) on OSWorld-G, 8ScreenSpot-Pro and ScreenSpot-v2, on which the constants were developed, and on held-out UI-Vision. Our conclusions are limited to these two backbones and four benchmarks and do not extend to agentic tasks in which grounding is one step of a longer episode. On Qwen3-VL-32B OSWorld-G, the comparison with MVP rests on its published number and is not paired. 

Cost. AdaGround reads 3.5 to 3.9 times the vi-sual tokens of the fixed 4× crop and 6 to 12 times those of the single pass. With sequential calls, its latency is 3.4 to 4.2 times that of the fixed crop (Appendix A.4). The extra compute pays off where targets are small. On ScreenSpot-v2, whose targets are large, AdaGround matches the fixed crop at a higher cost. 

Components. A second crop fixed at TNZ’s me-dian geometry is not significantly different from TNZ in arbitration on OSWorld-G and ScreenSpot-Pro, nor in the full pipeline on any benchmark, at fewer visual tokens (Appendix B.6). TNZ assumes that the model answers the box query with a box. A model that returns a point instead gets no zoom from TNZ. A target that falls outside an over-tight view is rejected by verification and left to the fixed 

4× chain and re-grounding. 

Verifier and abstention. One verifier score con-trols acceptance, re-grounding, arbitration and ab-stention, so a verifier that prefers a plausible but wrong element passes that preference to every deci-sion it controls. Many of its decisions are between scores near 1 (Appendix B.1). The joint absten-tion rule is set for few false refusals ( 0.8% and 

0.4% ; Appendix B.2) and catches 15 and 8 of the 

54 absent-target instructions of OSWorld-G. 

Structure. CSG covers spreadsheet cells but does not handle slider positions or text-cursor place-ment. On these cells, the single-pass answer does as well as the composed one (Appendix B.1). 

Ethical Considerations 

AdaGround is an inference-time procedure that trains no model and collects no new data. All ex-periments use the screenshots and instructions of four public GUI grounding benchmarks and in-volve no human subjects. Better grounding makes GUI agents more capable, including agents that act without supervision. A wrong click can trig-ger an unintended, possibly irreversible action, and capable agents can be misused to automate interac-tions that services intend for humans. Abstention removes one failure mode but does not replace user confirmation before consequential actions.

A.2 Prompt Template and Output Parsing Template structure. Every grounding pass uses the grounding system prompt of Appendix A.3, one image and the benchmark instruction I verbatim. The image is the whole screenshot or a crop, at the area factor of its stage, and no chain-of-thought or reasoning suffix is added. The box, verification, existence and program queries use the system mes-sage “You are a helpful assistant.” and the strings of Appendix A.3. 

Output format and parsing. The point query re-turns a pair (α, β ) on the grid [0 , 1,000] 2 of the rendered view. The parser takes the first pair writ-ten as (x, y ), provided every such pair has inte-ger values. It maps this pair to the screenshot as 

(⌊x0 + α w C /1000 ⌋, ⌊y0 + β h C /1000 ⌋), where 

(x0, y 0) and wC × hC are the crop’s actual corner and size in original pixels (the whole screenshot for a whole-screen view). This is Eq. (4) with-out its dependence on the rendered size, so neither the rounding to the 32 -pixel grid nor the pixel cap enters the mapping. Every crop and window is centred on the current answer and shifted inside the screenshot, hence the actual corner rather than 

p − d/ 2. A pass without such a pair yields no point, and its stage keeps the answer it had. Accordingly, TNZ does not zoom when the coarse pass fails, a zoom stage keeps its starting answer, re-grounding keeps the answer of its previous step, and a header grounding keeps its whole-screen point. The box query returns "bbox_2d": [x 1, y 1, x 2, y 2] on the same grid. Its shorter side, rescaled to original pixels, is ˆs.

Determinism and reproducibility. Outputs are not bit-exact across runs, because the continuous batching of vLLM (Kwon et al., 2023) can change the batch in which a request is computed. from 0.01 to 0.2. At θe = 0 .03 it recalls 27 .8% [17 .6, 40 .9] and 14 .8% [7 .7, 26 .6] of the absent tar-gets at false-refusal rates of 0.8% [0 .3, 2.0] and 

0.4% [0 .1, 1.4] (Wilson intervals). On Qwen3-VL-32B the existence rule at θe = 0 .01 reaches a higher overall accuracy, but with 19 false absten-tions. The rule also works without AdaGround. Ap-plied to the answers of the fixed 4× crop and of MVP on OSWorld-G (Table 13, superscript b), it raises them to 73 .8% (+3 .0 [+1 .3, +5 .0] ) and 

73 .0% (+3 .2 [+1 .5, +5 .0] ) on Qwen3-VL-8B. On 32B it raises the fixed crop to 78 .5% (+1 .4[+0 .2, +2 .8] ). 

B.3 Quality of the Size Estimate 

Both backbones return a box, and thus ˆs, on every sample. On ScreenSpot-Pro the estimate is coarse. Its median ratio to the true side is 1.2 (Qwen3-VL-8B) and 1.4 (32B), and its error is below the true 17 side on 82 .4% and 84 .1% of samples. Both back-bones therefore under-magnify on average (mean first-stage magnification 3.1 and 2.7, against 3.4

with the true s). On ScreenSpot-v2 the estimate is closer (median ratio 0.8 and 0.9) and both over-magnify slightly ( 1.8 and 1.7 against 1.5). TNZ is robust to this error. With the true s, the arbitrated accuracy changes by −0.6 to −0.2 points, and ev-ery interval contains zero (Qwen3-VL-8B). The estimate is not corrected, and a target outside an over-tight view can only be rejected by verification, not recovered. 

B.4 Design Choices 

Removing one component of AdaGround at a time gives three significant differences, all of them losses. They come from abstention on OSWorld-G with both backbones and from re-grounding on Qwen3-VL-8B ScreenSpot-Pro. A looser re-grounding threshold ( θr = 0 .9) changes accuracy by at most one sample net in every cell. Accuracy is not sensitive to the target span and changes by at most 0.6 points at τ = 2 and τ = 4 . Within TNZ, the first zoom carries most of the gain over the coarse point. The negative feedback in re-grounding (the crossed-out location and the ex-clusion note) changes which answers re-grounding returns, but overall accuracy differs significantly on no benchmark. 

B.5 Chain Arbitration 

Arbitration (Eq. (7)) is significantly above the better chain on ScreenSpot-Pro, and significantly above a random choice in four of the six cells (Table 8). The gain comes from the samples on which exactly one chain is right. On Qwen3-VL-8B ScreenSpot-Pro, both chains are right on 858

Ties and score precision. On Qwen3-VL-8B,

E.1 Definition, Relation to Area Measures, and Pitch Sensitivity 

The token span σ = sk/π (Eq. (1), with π = 32 

for Qwen3-VL (Bai et al., 2025a)) is measured in the rendered view. An area measure, the effective tokens on the target reff = T (AI ) · At/A I , is de-fined on the whole screenshot instead. Here At

and AI are the areas of the target and the screen-shot, and T (AI ) is the number of visual tokens of the whole screenshot. It satisfies reff ≈ σ2 for a square target at k = 1 . A 4× up-sampled crop leaves At/A I unchanged but doubles σ. This is why σ, and not the area ratio, predicts the effect of a zoom. For example, a 24 -pixel target on a 

3840 ×2160 screenshot spans 0.4 tokens on TNZ’s coarse view ( k ≈ 0.5), 0.75 at k = 1 and 1.5 after a 4× crop. Eq. (2) with the constants of Table 5 solves k = clip(3 ·32 /24 , 1, 4) = 4 and lands the target on 3.0.

Pitch sensitivity. The pitch π only fixes the unit. What matters is that size is measured after render-ing. A gate on original pixels is a different rule, because the same target is rendered at different scales on different screens.

E.3 The 2×2 Control and ScreenSpot-v2 

A crop also discards the screen around the target, a cost the regularity does not describe. A 2×2

control over {whole screen, crop } × { native, 4×} 

places both the benefit and the cost in the crop. Rendering the whole screen at four times the area raises the median view scale from 1.01 to 1.76 ,so the median span grows 1.7× with no context lost. Yet accuracy changes by only +0 .4 points on ScreenSpot-Pro ( p = 0 .72 , at 2.2 times the visual tokens) and −0.7 on ScreenSpot-v2 ( p = 0 .23 ). Cropping at native scale changes them by +8 .1

and −3.5 points. Span bought without restricting the view brings no gain. The crop both raises the span and removes candidate locations, and σ says which effect dominates. On ScreenSpot-v2, TNZ’s loose first-stage criterion fires on 92 .5% and 91 .8% 

of samples (Qwen3-VL-8B and 32B). Gated accep-tance keeps the zoomed answer on only 60 .8% and 

58 .2% , so cTNZ stays level with the single pass (Table 4). 

E.4 What a Well-Placed Crop Leaves Behind 

On ScreenSpot-Pro, an oracle square crop centred on the ground truth resolves only 287 of the single pass’s 787 failures and breaks 50 correct answers. With the square 4× crop, 322 of the 618 remaining failures ( 52% ) have the target inside the crop. In these cases the model saw the target and committed to another location. E.6 From the Analysis to the Design 

(1) The steep climb below about three tokens sets the span τ of every zoom (Eq. (2)). (2) A crop gains most below one token and nothing significant above three, so a missed zoom costs more than an unnecessary one.
