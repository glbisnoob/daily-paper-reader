# PointerBench: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Fine-Grained GUI Grounding

, Saurabh Pandey 2, Alexander Florian Jercher 2, Maximilian Schilling 21Sapienza University of Rome 2Warmwind AG 

# Abstract 

Multimodal Large Language Models have shown promising performance in computer-use tasks, from planning user workflows to grounding natural-language actions to precise UI targets. Existing end-to-end benchmarks primarily empha-size long-horizon task completion, while GUI grounding benchmarks remain focused on conventional controls and a limited set of professional interfaces. We introduce P OINTER BENCH , a diagnostic suite of 1,500 screenshot–instruction pairs probing three complementary capabilities: thin sub-element identification and geometric grounding in spreadsheets (P OINTER BENCH -Sheets), character-level grounding in long-text documents (P OINTER BENCH -Text), and mixed GUI grounding across 100 professional applications (P OINTER BENCH -Pro). We care-fully curate each task with pixel-exact target geometry and evaluate both general-purpose multimodal models and 39 specialist GUI grounding configurations. We find text grounding to be the most challenging setting, with Claude Fable 5, GPT-5.6 Sol, and the leading native specialist UI-Venus-2-9B achieving 69 .6% ,

53 .4% , and 25 .8% success on P OINTER BENCH -Text despite respective 83 .2% ,

77 .7% , and 54 .5% average success across P OINTER BENCH . These results re-veal substantial headroom in fine-grained GUI grounding across frontier and specialist models. We publicly release the benchmark and evaluation code at 

https://github.com/warmwindOS/pointerbench .

# 1 Introduction 

Computer-use agents have emerged as promising day-to-day assistants, automating workflows across browsers, desktop applications, mobile devices, and specialized professional software [Deng et al., 2023, Zhou et al., 2024, Xie et al., 2024, Rawles et al., 2025, Li et al., 2025]. Multimodal agents interleave reasoning and action [Yao et al., 2023], combining long-horizon planning [Erdogan et al., 2025], external tool use [Schick et al., 2023, Qin et al., 2024], and visual grounding [Cheng et al., 2024, Gou et al., 2025]. For tasks such as “open the meeting chat” or “paste cell B14 to cell E15”, successful execution requires both selecting an appropriate action sequence and grounding each step to precise screen coordinates or regions [Zheng et al., 2024, Gou et al., 2025]. This remains challenging in modern graphical user interfaces (GUIs), which often contain dense and visually ambiguous interaction targets, as well as in high-resolution professional applications with small target regions [Li et al., 2025, Wu et al., 2025]. Existing evaluations measure complementary but incomplete aspects of this problem. Interactive benchmarks such as Online-Mind2Web, WebArena, and OSWorld evaluate long-horizon task com-pletion in websites or executable desktop environments [Xue et al., 2025, Zhou et al., 2024, Xie et al., 2024]. They are essential for studying planning and recovery, but end-to-end success conflates failures in reasoning, grounding, execution, and environment control. Static benchmarks such as

ScreenSpot and ScreenSpot-Pro isolate localization and have accelerated progress on visual GUI agents [Cheng et al., 2024, Li et al., 2025]. Yet they lack fine-grained actions such as placing a cursor on a spreadsheet boundary, between two characters, or around a narrowly specified text span. Rapid progress on these benchmarks motivates complementary tests that vary the geometry of the requested action, diagnosing an agent’s ability to perform precise control. We introduce P OINTER BENCH , a fine-grained diagnostic benchmark comprising three 500-example tasks designed to diagnose these missing capabilities (Figure 1). POINTER BENCH -Sheets probes cells, headers, colors, relative references, edges, resize handles, and corners under varied sheet layouts. 

POINTER BENCH -Text evaluates points and bounding boxes over words, characters, punctuation, carets, and invoice fields in six languages. POINTER BENCH -Pro covers icon, text, and mixed targets across 100 professional applications. All inputs use a fixed 1024 ×768 coordinate frame and ship with executable scorers. Our contributions are twofold: (i) a diagnostic benchmark suite that isolates structural, textual, and professional GUI grounding through three complementary tasks with precisely defined target geometry; and (ii) a comprehensive evaluation spanning both general-purpose multimodal models and specialist GUI grounding configurations, revealing non-trivial rank reversals across subsets and a persistent bottleneck in character-level and region-level textual grounding. 

# 2 Related Work 

Interactive computer-use benchmarks. Mind2Web contains over 2,000 open-ended web tasks across 137 websites and records the corresponding action sequences [Deng et al., 2023]. WebArena provides reproducible, functional websites for evaluating long-horizon task execution [Zhou et al., 2024], while OSWorld extends this paradigm to real web and desktop applications with execution-based evaluators [Xie et al., 2024]. AndroidWorld similarly evaluates autonomous agents over real mobile applications [Rawles et al., 2025]. Recent work further studies the separation between high-level planning and low-level execution in long-horizon agents [Erdogan et al., 2025]. These benchmarks test complete interactive agents, whereas P OINTER BENCH deliberately removes temporal planning to isolate whether an already-specified action can be grounded precisely. 

GUI grounding. ScreenSpot introduced screenshot-only grounding across mobile, desktop, and web interfaces [Cheng et al., 2024], while ScreenSpot-Pro extends this setting to dense, high-resolution professional applications [Li et al., 2025]. OSWorld-G further decomposes computer-use grounding into recognition, text matching, layout reasoning, and precise manipulation [Xie et al., 2025]. UI-Vision provides fine-grained offline evaluation across 83 desktop applications through ele-ment grounding, layout grounding, and action prediction [Nayak et al., 2025], while FineState-Bench evaluates state-conditioned grounding and exact GUI state setting across desktop, web, and mobile interfaces [Ji et al., 2026]. Alongside these benchmarks, specialized GUI agents [Qin et al., 2025, Wang et al., 2025, Liu et al., 2026b, Zhou et al., 2025] and grounding models [Gou et al., 2025, Gu et al., 2025, Venus Team, 2026b,a, Chen et al., 2026] have substantially improved localization on 2conventional controls. Complementary advances include grounding architectures [Wu et al., 2025, Ouyang et al., 2026, Jiang et al., 2026], reinforcement learning methods [Yang et al., 2025, Tang et al., 2026, Liu et al., 2026a, Yuan et al., 2025], and inference-time prediction aggregation [Zhang et al., 2026, Du et al., 2026]. P OINTER BENCH complements these efforts by systematically iso-lating specific sub-element geometries under controlled, pixel-exact generation: thin spreadsheet boundaries, character- and caret-level text locations, and tightly bounded regions, while P OINTER -BENCH -Pro broadens coverage to 100 professional interfaces. This provides a reproducible diagnostic of grounding errors independently of temporal planning and environment execution. 

# 3 PointerBench 

Task design. In interactive environments, a grounding error may move an agent into an unintended state and propagate through subsequent actions [Xie et al., 2024, 2025]. Consider, for example, a spreadsheet task requiring an agent to resize a column or select a cell of a particular color. Although simple to specify in language, these actions require different forms of fine-grained grounding: the former requires localizing a thin boundary between adjacent columns, whereas the latter requires identifying a visually specified region within a repetitive grid. POINTER BENCH isolates these capabilities from long-horizon planning through three complementary 500-example suites: P OINTER BENCH -Sheets targets structural and geometric grounding, P OINTER -BENCH -Text targets fine-grained textual grounding, and P OINTER BENCH -Pro evaluates grounding across heterogeneous professional interfaces. Each benchmark instance consists of a 1024 ×768 

screenshot, a natural-language instruction, and a target expressed in absolute pixel coordinates. Depending on the task, the required output is either a point or a bounding box. Point predictions are successful when they fall inside the annotated target region. Bounding-box predictions require at least 0.90 target coverage and 0.70 prediction precision; malformed outputs are counted as failures. We adopt a standardized 1024 ×768 resolution for two reasons: first, fine-grained spatial capabilities, such as localizing 1–3 px spreadsheet dividers, inter-character carets, and individual glyphs, become substantially harder at higher resolutions as relative target area shrinks; second, higher resolutions markedly increase the time taken by a model to reason. Downscaling to 1024 ×768 makes evaluation computationally efficient while isolating sub-element geometric precision. Dataset construction and reproducibility details are provided in the Appendix. 3.2 Specialist GUI Grounding Models 

Because dedicated GUI agents are trained under model-specific coordinate conventions, we evaluate 39 open-weight specialist GUI grounding configurations (2B–32B parameters) using each model’s native grounding prompt and parser (Table 2; full 39-run leaderboard in Appendix G). Across the method families in Table 2, Multi-View Prediction (MVP), which aggregates predictions across multiple augmented views at inference time, is the leading UI specialist, scoring best on every track with its 32B backbone ( 68 .4% Sheets, 34 .0% Text, 75 .4% Pro, 59 .3% Avg.). Behind MVP-32B, a rank reversal occurs across suites among trailing models: UI-Venus-2-9B (pairing domain-specific grounding supervision with reinforcement learning) performs second best on Pro ( 73 .6% , surpassing all single-view 32B models), while GTA1-32B (an RL-trained grounding model that directly predicts click coordinates) does so on Text ( 29 .6% ) and overall ( 54 .9% ), and OpenCUA-32B (trained on large-scale multi-OS trajectories) on Sheets ( 66 .0% ). Considering smaller models ( < 9B), MAI-UI-8B (an instruction-tuned desktop/mobile grounding agent) performs best at 51 .3% average, followed closely by MVP-8B ( 51 .2% ), UI-Venus-1.5-8B ( 51 .0% ), and GTA1-7B ( 50 .1% ), ahead of OpenCUA-7B (47 .3% ), UI-Venus-Ground-7B ( 46 .2% ), and UI-TARS-1.5-7B ( 40 .6% ). 

3.3 Per-Suite Analysis POINTER BENCH -Sheets. POINTER BENCH -Sheets isolates the spatial structure of spreadsheet interfaces, where successful interaction may depend on reasoning over a grid rather than simply recognizing a conventional GUI element. The suite includes cells, headers, color-based targets, relative references, thin boundaries and resize handles, and cell corners, separating conventional element localization from structural and geometric grounding. Sheets is the strongest suite for the leading models, with Opus, Sol, and Fable reaching 98 .8% , 97 .8% , and 97 .6% , respectively. Conventional cell and header targets are localized almost perfectly, while performance drops on finer geometric interactions: Fable reaches 78 .1% on row-resize handles, showing that near-perfect aggregate performance can still conceal errors on thin structural elements. 

POINTER BENCH -Text. POINTER BENCH -Text probes fine-grained textual grounding through words, characters, punctuation, caret positions, and tightly specified regions. Targets may occupy only a narrow portion of a line or lie between adjacent glyphs, with point and bounding-box tasks spanning six languages and multiple document surfaces. Despite strong conventional localization (e.g., UI-Venus-2-9B reaches 89 .0% –100 .0% on spreadsheet cells/headers, vs. 31 .6% –55 .8% on borders/resize handles and 3.0% on relative offsets), POINTER BENCH -Text is the primary bottleneck across all specialist configurations, with no specialist exceeding 34 .0% (MVP-32B 34 .0% , GTA1-32B 29 .6% , UI-Venus-2-9B 25 .8% , MAI-UI-8B 23 .2% ). Word localization is easiest: Fable reaches 90 .2% , followed by Opus ( 83 .9% ), Sol ( 76 .8% ), and top specialists GTA1-32B ( 70 .5% ) and MVP-32B ( 69 .6% ). Accuracy drops sharply at finer granularity: Opus and Sol reach 51 .9% /48 .1% on characters and 39 .1% /56 .5% on punctuation, while top UI specialists fall to 11 .1–18 .5% on characters and 22 .5–34 .2% on carets ( 19 .3% for MAI-UI-8B). Region grounding, requiring tight bounds over text spans or fields, remains challenging for frontier models (Fable 56 .0% , Opus 50 .7% ). Specialists whose native output is a point are scored on that point and therefore receive 0.0% on all 134 region tasks. When a model returns a box, that box is scored: GUI-G2-7B [Tang et al., 2026] reaches 12 .7% on Region, and UI-Venus-Ground-7B reaches 

11 .2% (24 .6% overall on P OINTER BENCH -Text). Figure 2 further decomposes caret grounding: placements immediately before, after, or between words are non-trivial, with no frontier model exceeding 50% accuracy and UI specialists remaining below 24% , whereas coarse structural targets (line start, paragraph start/end) are localized more reliably. 

POINTER BENCH -Pro. POINTER BENCH -Pro evaluates grounding across visually heterogeneous professional interfaces. It spans 100 applications targeting icons, text elements, and interface controls such as dropdowns, list items, sliders, tabs, and panels. Performance is consistent among the leading models, with Fable, Opus, Sol, and Luna reaching 82 .4% , 81 .2% , 82 .0% , and 79 .4% , respectively. Text controls are easiest, followed by other interface controls, while icons are hardest. Icons account for 57 .3% of pooled failures despite representing only 38 .0% of examples, indicating that a major remaining challenge lies in mapping an intended action to the correct visual control and highlighting icon understanding and UI semantics as important targets for improving computer-use agents. 

Coordinate-frame compliance. Gemini 3.7 Flash and Qwen3.8 Max predominantly return coordi-nates in a 0–1000 frame despite being explicitly instructed to use absolute 1024 × 768 coordinates; the exact prompt is reported in the Appendix. Their official scores are therefore 3.1% and 2.8% ,respectively. Uniformly converting parseable predictions to the requested frame raises these to 78 .1% 

and 55 .4% , placing Gemini within the performance range of the benchmark’s leading models. This suggests that grounding performance can depend strongly on model-native coordinate conventions and motivates evaluation under normalized output representations. 

# 4 Conclusion 

We introduced P OINTER BENCH , a diagnostic benchmark for fine-grained GUI grounding across spreadsheet structure, text, and professional interfaces. Evaluation across both frontier multimodal systems and specialist GUI grounding configurations reveals that aggregate performance can hide markedly different grounding capabilities. Leading models reliably localize conventional spreadsheet and application controls, yet remain challenged by thin structural elements; textual grounding degrades as interaction moves from words to characters, carets, and precisely bounded regions; and professional-interface failures concentrate disproportionately on icons. By exposing these failures at the level of individual grounding primitives, P OINTER BENCH provides a targeted test bed for measuring and improving precise visual control in computer-use agents. 5References

# A Benchmark Construction and Reproducibility 

POINTER BENCH contains three 500-example suites with a shared interface: a 1024 ×768 screenshot, a natural-language instruction, and pixel-exact target geometry. Sheets and Text are generated programmatically from controlled interface descriptions, while Pro is assembled from a larger collection of generated professional interfaces. The released metadata stores the instruction, target geometry, answer type, and diagnostic tags used by the scorers. We open source the benchmark metadata and evaluation script required to score it. 

POINTER BENCH -Sheets. Each example is produced by sampling a visible spreadsheet grid, layout, visual style, and target type, and then rendering the resulting interface together with its instruction and target coordinates. The visible sheet is varied through row and column offsets, style changes, and optional application chrome, preventing a fixed correspondence between semantic locations and screen coordinates. Targets include cells and headers as well as colors, relative references, resize handles, edges, and corners. Generation is deterministic, yielding one distinct screenshot and metadata record per example. 8POINTER BENCH -Text. Text examples are rendered from controlled document surfaces with exact glyph geometry. Tasks cover words, characters, punctuation, caret positions, text spans, and structured document fields, with 366 point targets and 134 bounding-box targets. The latter include word-, line-, character-, paragraph-, and invoice-field regions. The language mix is fixed to 50% English and 10% 

each German, French, Spanish, Italian, and Dutch. Generation is deterministic, and examples are retained only when they identify a unique target. 

POINTER BENCH -Pro. Pro is assembled from generated professional-application interfaces. Can-didate targets are sampled across applications and balanced so that each of the 100 applications contributes five examples. The resulting suite contains 190 icon targets, 154 text targets, and 156 other interface controls. For icon examples, instructions explicitly request the icon itself to avoid ambiguity with nearby text labels. Application name, category, platform, and source identifier are retained in the released metadata, allowing each benchmark example to be traced to its originating interface. The final selection is deterministic and reproducible.

All models receive the same screenshot–instruction pairs and return either a point or a bounding box in the benchmark coordinate frame. # H Additional Limitations 

POINTER BENCH isolates one-step GUI grounding from temporal planning, recovery, and environment dynamics. Its scores should therefore not be interpreted as a complete measure of computer-use-agent capability; conversely, success on an interactive benchmark does not guarantee robustness to the fine-grained targets considered here. All inputs use a fixed 1024 × 768 frame and absolute pixel coordinates. The benchmark does not directly measure robustness to arbitrary display resolutions, resizing policies, or alternative coordinate representations. As with other public benchmarks evaluated through closed models, we cannot independently verify whether benchmark examples or closely related material have appeared in proprietary pretraining corpora. POINTER BENCH -Sheets and P OINTER BENCH -Text are generated specifically for the benchmark, while P OINTER BENCH -Pro uses generated professional interfaces, reducing direct overlap with established GUI grounding datasets but not eliminating the possibility of exposure to similar interfaces or tasks. 16
