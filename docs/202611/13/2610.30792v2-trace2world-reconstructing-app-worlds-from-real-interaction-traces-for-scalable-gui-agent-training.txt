FROM REAL INTERACTION TRACES FOR SCALABLE
GUI AGENT TRAINING
Zezhou Wang1,2∗, Hanzhang Zhou2‡, Panrong Tong2, Quyu Kong2, Pengxiang Li2,

1State Key Laboratory of Novel Software Technology, Nanjing University, Nanjing 210023, China

ABSTRACT
Scaling GUI reinforcement learning requires environments that preserve real-app
behavior. Yet coupling behavior inference with coding can turn partial traces
into runnable mocks with incorrect state transitions. We introduce Trace2World,
which separates global behavior reconstruction from implementation. It reconciles
traces into a shared, source-linked app model, then compiles an executable environ-
ment. Independent replay of source workflows localizes mismatches for bounded
implementation repair. Validated worlds then support learner-adaptive task and
environment augmentation within source-supported behavior. From 1,570 traces,
Trace2World constructs 50 environments. On 200 workflows across ten apps, 91.1%
are reproduced end to end, versus 59.8% for the strongest matched task-local base-
line. Source-grounded validation retains 94.0% of simulated training gains on real
devices, versus 54.7% under self-validation. Learner guidance raises benchmark
success from 56.9% to 61.1%. Training in Trace2World further improves the model
by 20.2 points on our benchmark, 5.4–16.2 points across four external GUI bench-
marks, and 16.3 points on 100 real-device tasks. Reliable reconstruction separates
what behavior source evidence supports from whether the implementation preserves
it. Project page: https://tongyi-mai.github.io/trace2app.

INTRODUCTION
Online reinforcement learning improves GUI agents through repeated interaction (Bai et al., 2024; Qi
et al., 2025; Qin et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025). Real apps are hard to reset, and exploratory actions can
change remote state or commit transactions (Rawles et al., 2025; Xie et al., 2024; Kong et al., 2026;
Yan et al., 2025). Simulators offer controlled starts, yet training value depends on transfer: visual
(Rusu et al., 2017) or dynamics (Peng et al., 2018) gaps can break learned policies. For pixel-based
GUI agents, these gaps lie in what the policy sees and what its actions do, so we use fidelity to mean
preserving the source app’s task-relevant observations and action semantics, including state, data,
intermediate effects, and outcomes, not only its look or final value.

Partial paths do not define the app. Each trace exposes
only one path from one initial state (Rawles et al., 2023;
Deng et al., 2023): Home screens showing Xi’an and Bei-
jing may be two pages or one page in two city states, while
one control can have state-dependent effects (Section 3.2).
A page-transition graph records what was observed, not the
state condition that explains it, so coding before reconcil-
iation hardens one interpretation into the app. Across our
matched builds of this family, routes reach 50.4–94.8% of endpoints yet preserve only 16.7–59.8% of
source workflows (Table 1; Figure 1a and Appendix F.1).
Passing tests do not establish fidelity. An implementation and the tests derived from the same
interpretation can agree while both diverge from the source (Barr et al., 2015). In Meituan, tapping
Disable quick pay opens a confirmation without changing the saved setting; our PhoneWorld-style
reconstruction disables it immediately yet passes a final-value check (Figure 2), rewarding a policy
for a transition the real app does not perform. Thus the terminal value alone cannot show that the
workflow matches the source.
Both failures arise when an ambiguous page identity, state value, or action effect becomes a coding
choice before the traces are reconciled or an independent check can contest it. The remaining
challenge is therefore not a better coding loop but a question no existing route poses:
Which app semantics do partial traces actually support, and what evidence may accept an
implementation of them?
Trace2World answers with two boundaries, semantic commitment before implementation and
source acceptance afterward: it resolves open-ended app semantics before construction, then

Our contributions follow this sequence:
1. Global behavior reconstruction as the semantic contract. Source-linked semantics are fixed
from evidence before any code exists. From 1,570 partial traces, we build 50 shared worlds; on
200 workflows across ten apps, 91.1% pass all six source checks versus 59.8% for the strongest
existing route, our matched task-local GUI-GENESIS-style build.
2. Source-grounded detection with bounded local repair. Independent replay of every original
workflow checks six source obligations, apart from builder tests and RL rewards, and shows that
every existing route reaches endpoints it cannot preserve. Replacing replay with self-validation
lowers the share passing all six from 91.1% to 79.1% and real-device gain retention from 94.0%
to 54.7%.
3. Validated worlds that index and expand cheaply. Forty validated environments supply 1,280
seed and 768 learner-guided tasks within fixed source support. Learner guidance raises benchmark
success from 56.9% to 61.1%; our final policy improves on four external GUI benchmarks and by
16.3 points on real devices.

RELATED WORK
Environment construction and semantic fidelity.
Constructed GUI environments differ less
in how they are coded than in where their semantics come from and what evidence establishes
correctness (Table A1). AndroidWorld executes the real application, while MobileGym, WebArena,
and AppWorld author environment behavior by hand (Rawles et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2026; Zhou et al.,
2024; Trivedi et al., 2024). CUA-Gym synthesizes topic-based mocks together with their verifiers
(Wang et al., 2026), GUI-GENESIS builds one task-local site and reward per trace (Cao et al., 2026),
and PhoneWorld, in concurrent work, codes reusable Android mocks from recovered screen types
and transitions and closes representative flows by human side-by-side QA (Liu et al., 2026). These
coding-agent routes commit semantics inside implementation. Trace2World changes the semantic

contract: every trace of an app is reconciled into one source-linked model before any code exists,
unresolved conflicts stay outside executable scope, and independent replay of every source workflow

Experience supply and evolving tasks.
A separate line of work adapts training experience once
an environment exists. Online GUI RL and task synthesis draw new experience from interaction
(Bai et al., 2024; Qi et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025; Xue et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2025; Shi et al.,
2025). Qwen-UI-Agent synthesizes tasks, environments, and executable verifiers and iterates on
failure analysis (Zhou et al., 2026); Agent-World uses capability gaps to evolve tool-environment
curricula (Dong et al., 2026). Learnability-driven curricula adapt task selection to learner competence
(Sukhbaatar et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2019; Dennis et al., 2020; Jiang et al., 2021). The pipeline
connects the two lines through a validated app world: semantics are reconstructed and accepted once
per app, while learner feedback changes only which supported behavior is practiced.

TRACE2WORLD
3.1
FROM TRACES TO ONE SHARED APP
A source trace records one successful human workflow on a real app: its instruction, observations,
actions, terminal criterion, and collection metadata. It exposes only one path from one initial state.
Trace2World reconciles traces from the same app into one shared app model: common pages and
entities, mutable state, reusable data, task-specific initial values, and state-dependent action effects,
each linked to supporting source steps. Ambiguous or unsupported behavior remains non-executable.
This model is the semantic commitment boundary between source evidence and browser code.
To separate reconstruction, implementation, and task authoring, Trace2World maintains three objects.
The app model S is reconstructed once from source traces and then frozen; the executable package
Er is the browser world and the only object repair edits; and the task-facing index Ir is a read-only
projection of a validated package for task authoring. Source replay checks Er against the recordings,
while learner feedback changes which supported experience is generated. This separation yields one
resettable environment per app with task-specific initial state rather than task-specific semantics;
definitions and the algorithm appear in Appendix A.

Two observations may look different because they show
different pages, different values of the same state, or one
entity rendered on different surfaces. The pipeline first
matches repeated pages, action hosts, and entities across
traces using their containment, routes, lifecycle, and ob-

Actions that appear identical can still have different ef-
fects. The model links each candidate effect to the source
step that shows its precondition and result. If observed
state explains a difference, the app model records that
condition. If no observed condition explains conflicting
effects, it keeps the conflict unresolved instead of choosing
one interpretation. Concretely, each supported interaction
has a page and control, an action and guard, state or data
reads and writes, a destination, and a visible postcondi-
tion. The resulting action rule is reused across workflows.
A final support check requires complete page and entity mappings, compatible effects, their data
dependencies, and coverage of the cited evidence (Appendices A.1 and A.3).

3.3
BUILDING AND VALIDATING THE ENVIRONMENT
Bounded API calls compile the supported part of the app model into page instructions and then into
an initial standalone, DOM-backed browser package; no open-ended coding agent builds the world.
A central state kernel owns business state and applies guarded updates; page code chooses only
rendering, routes, and storage. Unsupported controls stay visible but inert, and unsupported clicks or
failed guards leave business state unchanged. Code checks confirm that required pages, resources,
hosts, bindings, and resets are present; they establish that the package runs, not that it matches the
source app (Appendix B.1).
Source validation replays each recorded workflow from reset in the package. A read-only checker
compares the original trace with the resulting information, intermediate effects, and terminal state
without seeing the app model, generated verifier, or repair log; contract probes also check reset,
repeated actions, guards, and supported alternatives. Detection and editing are separate roles: neither
check edits anything, and each failure becomes a defect packet naming the step and the expected and
observed state.
Compilation gives repair a concrete executable hypothesis. Reconstruction jointly infers identity,
state, data, and action dynamics from partial evidence; repair localizes mismatches within the package.
A bounded agent receives the package, defect packet, and source evidence, edits only the package,
and reruns both checks. It may correct state updates, control handlers, or other implementation
errors exposed by the source, but cannot change the frozen app model or add unsupported behavior.
Freezing reduces joint revision of semantics and implementation to local repair of one artifact under
a fixed contract.
Repair remains bounded by reconstructed support. A workflow that cannot pass without changing the
app model is excluded after three repair waves; later model revisions begin a separate construction
revision. The evaluator for RL reward and held-out success is separate from source replay (Appen-
dices B.2–B.3), so validation can reject a workflow accepted by the package’s own success check
(Figure 2). After trace collection, acceptance requires no human QA; human fidelity labels serve only
the independent evaluation audit (Appendix E.1.4).
3.4
LEARNER-GUIDED TASK GENERATION
After source replay, Trace2World extracts a read-only index of the validated package’s surfaces,
entities, observable fields, supported operations, and already-modeled dependencies, retaining source
and implementation tags. The indexer reconstructs no behavior. Task generation queries the index
to compose tasks, rebind observed entities or initial states, and select combinations relevant to the
current learner (Figure 5). Candidates requiring unsupported app-level transitions, guards, or effects
are rejected.
A supported task may still need content or implementation absent from the package. Trace2World
instantiates schema-backed entities, values, and state or materializes already-supported operations as
a task slice. GUI execution and affected seed-task regressions check the expanded package before
the index is refreshed. Learner feedback may expose these gaps but cannot change the app model
(Appendices C and E.3.3). Augmentation thus amortizes one semantic reconstruction and source
acceptance across tasks.
A fixed external solver filters unsolvable candidates; four current-learner attempts estimate reward
SD, with values above 0.1 marking useful variation. Saturated candidates may be refined twice within
source support. Training on 1,280 seeds yields π1; two 384-task guided batches then yield π2 and π3
(the final policy). Task membership, environment revision, and evaluator freeze before each stage
(Appendix C.3).

EXPERIMENTS
The pipeline constructs one shared environment per app from 1,570 real-app traces across 50 apps.
Forty apps supply 1,280 seed and 768 learner-guided tasks for policy training; the other ten contribute
construction traces but no optimization tasks and form the unseen-app split. We test three questions:
(i) do reconstruction and independent source validation preserve recorded workflows on a fixed
200-workflow, ten-app set; (ii) do validated worlds support useful adaptive training on Trace2World-
Bench and four external GUI benchmarks; and (iii) do simulation gains transfer to real devices on 100
paired tasks across 20 policy-training apps? Qwen3.5-9B (Qwen Team, 2026) is trained with DAPO
(Yu et al., 2025) across three frozen stages; Qwen3.7-Plus is used only for solvability screening. Full
split, horizon, model-role, and training contracts appear in Appendices D.2, D.3, and D.4.
4.1
END-TO-END RECONSTRUCTION FIDELITY
Setup and metrics.
All conditions use the same 200 source workflows from the ten apps held
out from policy training, one per domain. We operationalize fidelity as workflow fidelity, the share
of source workflows that pass all six checks: reachability, surface, state, effect, terminal outcome,
and data. Equivalent routes and page partitions pass when they preserve the required decisions
and effects. A blinded GPT-5.6 Sol reviewer compares each anonymous execution with its source
recording and labels every check P/F; missing evidence is F, and rates are macro-averaged over apps
(Appendix E.1.1).
We reimplement Direct Agent, CUA-Gym, PhoneWorld-style, and GUI-GENESIS-style on the same
substrate, tools, runtime, and recordings, with matched model capability per builder role and each
route’s construction rules (Appendix E.1.2). Builder rows compare complete construction contracts;
only the controls isolate one factor: no-trace variants withhold the recordings, and self-validation
changes only the validation source (Table E1).

CUA-Gym, and PhoneWorld-style reach 50.4–57.0% of endpoints, yet only 16.7–22.4% of their
workflows pass all six checks (Table 1). The task-local GUI-GENESIS-style build reaches 94.8% but

The gap reflects where app behavior is committed. When semantic inference and implementation
are coupled, an uncertain page identity, state value, or action effect becomes a coding choice before
conflicting source evidence is reconciled. A builder can therefore recover a reachable surface while
encoding the wrong state or effect behind it. Trace2World instead reconstructs source-linked behavior
before compilation and leaves unresolved conflicts outside executable support.
The ordering is not explained by a stronger constructor or reviewer bias. GPT-5.2 performs
Trace2World’s reconciliation and initial compilation, and GPT-5.5 only its verification and repair,
whereas compared builders receive GPT-5.5 in every role they define. Blind human relabeling
agrees with the automated reviewer on 90.2% of the same 1,200 packets and preserves the ordering
(Appendices D.2 and E.1.4).
More source evidence expands supported behavior without collapsing fidelity. Growing the
trace set of the same ten apps from 100 to 290 traces expands supported routes from 270 to 512 and
visual obligations from 553 to 1,076, while workflow fidelity, scored against each trace budget’s
own supported scope, moves only from 92.6% to 91.1% (Figure E3 in Appendix E.2.2). Across all
1,570 source workflows, 74.6% pass source replay on the first attempt and all close within the K = 3
automated repair cap without human QA (Appendix E.2.3). A seed build takes an estimated 3.6 hours
and 1,160 model calls per app, comparable to PhoneWorld-style and below GUI-GENESIS-style
(Appendix E.2.4).
4.2
SOURCE-GROUNDED VALIDATION
Setup and metrics.
The self-validation control changes only validation evidence under the same
construction stages and inputs (Table 1). We compare stage-1 policies on the same 100 paired
sim/device tasks, with matched goals, restorable starts, terminal criteria, and action budgets; the 50
entity/state rebindings and 50 held-out workflows never enter RL, and results average training seeds
(Appendix E.4.1).
Self-consistency does not establish source fidelity. Replacing source-trace validation with self-
validation lowers workflow fidelity from 91.1% to 79.1% while Reach stays near 98%. Independent
source rechecks reject 22/160 (13.8%) self-accepted executions versus 1/176 (0.6%) source-accepted
ones (Appendix E.1.3). A separate 400-execution audit isolates the mechanism: giving the same
model reviewer the source trace raises defect coverage from 43.2% to 68.4% and precision from
73.6% to 88.2% while flagging fewer clean executions (Appendix E.2.6).
Source-trace validation decides how much environment gain transfers. On the shared 100-task
panel, source validation retains 94.0% of its 16.7-pp simulated gain on real devices (15.7 pp), versus
54.7% of 17.7 pp under self-validation (9.7 pp), a 6.0-pp paired difference (seed SD 1.0). With
training matched and simulated gains nearly equal, the rise in simulation-only passes from 10.0% to
18.3% locates the transfer loss in behavior the simulator accepts but the device rejects.

Setup and metrics.
Four branches fork from π1 for the same 20 RL iterations (32–52), environment
revisions, sampler, and evaluator: learner-guided generation uses feedback; static generation removes
it from the same generator; entity rebinding substitutes seed-template entities; and seed continuation
redraws seed tasks, each with two 384-task training-only batches. Static and learner-guided tasks
also share H = 45; seed-based tasks use H = 30. Four attempts per candidate under its class budget
give current-policy difficulty (mean pass) and learning signal (reward SD above 0.1); the held-out
benchmark reports SR and PR.
Learner feedback makes tasks harder for the learner, not easier. Cross-surface composition,
lifecycle revisit, and conditional recovery become more common than in the 1,280 seeds, and the
dependency-heavy share rises from 46% to 71% across the two guided batches. The current learner’s
pass rate among validated candidates falls from 41% to 29% while the fixed solver’s moves only from
71% to 66%, and 194 of 200 audited accepted candidates are independently solvable by humans; the
new tasks recombine supported capabilities rather than add semantics (Appendices E.3.3 and E.3.2).
New tasks expand implementation, not semantics. The support gate rejects 77 and 90 of the 576
candidates proposed per guided round (Table E8), so selected tasks bind only indexed, supported
items. Of 611 terminal-reaching attempt cases on paths absent from source traces, 486 are source-
equivalent, 402 already executing and 84 materialized from supported edges; the remaining 125
ambiguous, invalid, or unresolved cases stay outside support (Appendix E.3.3).
Learner-conditioned generation supplies tasks with learning signal. Across the two batches,
76.8/75.3% of guided tasks carry learning signal versus 47.9/44.0% for the static control. The gap
precedes bounded refinement, which is sparse: only 14.1/14.5% of validated lineages enter it, and
about 60% of those become selectable within two rounds. Feedback thus changes which supported
tasks are proposed, not just which candidates are rescued (Figure 6a, Appendix E.3.2).
Learner-guided batches outperform matched continuations. At iteration 52, SR is 61.1% guided,
56.9% static, 55.3% entity rebinding, and 52.2% seed continuation. The two H = 45-matched
augmentation branches differ by 4.2 pp, isolating learner feedback (Figure 6b,c); external MobileGym
keeps the ordering (25.2±1.2 versus 22.2±1.3 SR). Budget does not substitute for feedback: training
on all 426/427 validated candidates for 54 iterations reaches 57.8% SR, and seed continuation matched
to the guided run’s 1,351 retained groups needs 79 iterations to reach 54.3% (Appendix E.3.2).

CONCLUSION
Trace2World separates two problems that agentic reconstruction conflates: global reasoning re-
constructs app behavior once from partial traces, leaving unresolved effects non-executable, while
later source mismatches become local repairs over one world that independent replay accepts. This
contract reaches 91.1% workflow fidelity versus 59.8% for the strongest existing route and keeps
94.0% of simulated gains on real devices versus 54.7% under self-validation. Reusing validated
worlds amortizes reconstruction and source validation across learner-guided tasks. The bottleneck for
scalable GUI RL is no longer producing runnable mocks but reconstructing app worlds whose state
and effects are fixed by evidence, checked on the source workflows, and trusted without human QA.

A.1
SOURCE-TRACE PREPROCESSING
Reconciliation consumes recorded traces, not raw recordings. Preprocessing selects the traces that
enter construction, gives every recorded step a typed disposition and a semantic description, and
extracts the visual resources those steps contain. It produces only trace- and step-linked local evidence:
canonical page identity, State fields, guards, and verifier predicates are decided later by reconciliation
and are never authored here.
Preprocessing covers the fixed 50-application construction corpus: 19 multi-service applications
contribute 950 traces and 31 single-service applications contribute 620, for 1,570 traces in total.
Figure A2 visualizes step dispositions. Table A2 then jointly reports trace selection and the visual-
evidence volume it produces. Resource extraction is summarized in text.
Trace selection and diversity control.
Operators record complete workflows on one real applica-
tion from a frozen app-level task catalog that varies goals, entry routes, entity bindings, launch states,
and supported compositions. A recording is admitted only when visible terminal evidence or an
independent receipt establishes its outcome at the recorded application revision; a recorder flag or a
generated description is insufficient, and agent-generated exploration is excluded. Admission certifies
the outcome, not the necessity of each intermediate action, so retries and corrections stay in the
evidence. Admitted recordings exceed the per-application budget, and selection keeps diversity rather
than volume: candidates must contain at least four in-app interaction steps once launch, terminal, and
handover rows are discounted; task instructions are embedded with the bge-large-zh-v1.5 encoder
(Xiao et al., 2023) and clustered, and traces are drawn to cover clusters while avoiding duplicate
action-signature prefixes. Across the corpus, this process retains the fixed 1,570-trace construction
set from 6,541 admitted recordings, a 24.0% retention rate (Table A2). Figure A1 instantiates the
diversity side of that trade on Meituan: halving the pool from 106 admitted candidates to 50 selected
traces leaves every occupied workflow bucket populated, and the selected traces carry 50 distinct
action signatures with no repeated three-action prefix.

C.1
TASK CANDIDATE AUTHORING
Task-facing index.
After a package passes source replay, the indexer extracts a read-only index
Ir = Π(Er, replay records, Sanchors) from the source-validated package Er; the frozen app model
contributes only stable identifiers, provenance, and support anchors, so the index never reads a stale
specification once repair has moved the package closer to the source. The index lists surface, host,
and control identifiers, entity schemas, observable fields, supported operation identifiers, observable
outcomes, implementation status, and source links; guards, effects, and destinations appear as
references to already-established operations rather than as a second copy of transition semantics. A
dependency index records producer, consumer, value type, entity scope, freshness, and the supported
discovery path that makes an instruction-implicit value observable. Reconstructing dynamics is the
modeling problem; the indexer does not reconstruct app behavior but extracts addressable structure
from the validated world and attaches references to established operations and dependencies, so it
has no semantic authority. Every emitted item must reference a supported relation and its evidence,
an item that cannot be resolved is dropped rather than inferred, and no transition, guard, or effect is
introduced. Because extraction is cheap, the index is refreshed after each instantiated task slice rather
than re-derived by modeling (Appendix G).
Candidate binding.
Task authoring reads this index and performs no structure extraction of its
own. A candidate binds its user goal and terminal value, launch state, business subtasks and decisions,
cross-state dependencies, evidence revisits, acceptable terminal set, reset boundary, and evaluator-
observable outcome to indexed items and their sources. A missing support binding rejects the
candidate; a binding whose item is supported but not yet implemented routes to implementation
rather than to reconciliation. Appendices G and G give the condensed indexing and task-authoring
instructions; Algorithm 5 owns screening and selection.
Cross-application benchmark authoring.
The learner-guided branch produces only single-app
training tasks. A separate pre-evaluation branch joins two source-validated app-local indexes through
typed, GUI-visible handoffs A →B →A. Its local requirements satisfy ReqA(c) ⊆ΩA

Difficulty follows the four capabilities in Figure 5: long-horizon, reasoning, memory/state, and recov-

C.3

A training stage is one pass over a frozen task batch; an RL iteration is one DAPO optimizer step
within that stage. The three stages use seed tasks, augmented batch 1, and augmented batch 2. For

attempts, and environment revisions before optimization. Appendix G specifies how prior practice
outcomes condition the next authoring pass. Between stages, supported implementation changes
must be reverified before the next freeze.

EVALUATOR ROLES AND NON-INTERFERENCE
We distinguish three checks. Construction-time validation verifies trace obligations on builder
executions. The environment task evaluator programmatically evaluates business-state checkpoints
to supply the RL reward and held-out SR/PR. The independent fidelity reviewer compares raw
source-trace evidence with generated executions and supplies binary reconstruction labels; it never
scores training or held-out rollouts.
The three outputs are not interchangeable: construction-time validation can trigger environment
repair, task-evaluator credit can train or score a policy, and fidelity-review labels can support only
the reconstruction study. Final evaluation and human annotations never return to construction, task
filtering, or checkpoint selection.

history. Six P/F diagnostics cover Structure (Reach and Surface), State (initialization/lifecycle, action
Effect, and Terminal outcome), and Data (entities, relations, and values). Missing or contradicted
required evidence is F; equivalent routes and page partitions are allowed when they preserve the
required decision and effect (Zhou et al., 2024; Chai et al., 2026). The all-six workflow verdict is P
only when every diagnostic passes. Every diagnostic uses the same tasks with no N/A label, and rates
first average within app and then equally across apps.
Execution and scope.
Evaluation starts from the native reset or declared task preset and permits
at most max(80, 3L) GUI actions for a source trace of length L. Up to two recollections address
stale or incomplete capture; the first complete packet is scored, while an absent valid packet remains
incomplete. The implemented behavior scope fixes which visible components may act for that
episode, so a correct NOT INTERACTIVE outside the task boundary is not a capability failure. State
invariance and the absence of unsupported effects serve as negative obligations when no positive
mutation is required.
Validation and records.
Builder-side validation is reapplied to the independently collected eval-
uation packet before the review label is exposed. This recheck is separate from construction-time
validation; the false-pass rate is the app-macro fraction of recheck-passing tasks whose all-six verdict
is F. Each final record binds the artifact and source trace digests, native start, packet, six labels, cited
observations, and decisive discrepancy. The full instruction and record schema remain in the artifact.

Why the profiles take this shape.
One interpretation is that the difficult commitment is semantic
rather than implementation-level. When semantic inference and implementation are coupled, an
uncertain page identity, state value, or action effect must become a concrete coding choice before
it can remain an explicit conflict, and later checks mainly test that implementation of the chosen
reading. Trace2World moves the commitment upstream: model-proposed relations are factorized into
explicit Structure, State, and Data, unresolved conflicts stay outside the compiled support, bounded
API compilation emits the initial package from the resulting page specifications, and agentic repair
edits that package without redefining their semantics. This is consistent with the high reach but low
workflow fidelity above and with Appendix F.1, where a task-local builder reproduces one workflow
exactly while task-local correctness does not determine state shared across workflows. The ordering
is also not explained by assigning Trace2World the stronger constructor: its reconciliation and initial
compilation run on GPT-5.2, whereas every compared builder receives GPT-5.5 in the construction
roles it defines (Appendix D.2). These remain mechanism-consistent interpretations of complete
construction contracts, not a stage-wise causal ablation.
A second factor may amplify the difficulty for native applications. A website ships its production client
to any crawler, and open pretraining corpora are correspondingly dominated by web content at trillion-
token scale (Soldaini et al., 2024). A real mobile application ships a binary: the largest Android
research collections distribute APK artifacts (Allix et al., 2016), while source-linked collections
necessarily cover the open-source subset (Liu et al., 2020), which excludes the large consumer
applications studied here. Rendered interaction is therefore the broadly available evidence of a real
application’s production semantics, which is what source traces provide. We treat this as an amplifier
rather than an explanation of Trace2World’s advantage: cross-trace ambiguity is a property of partial
evidence rather than of the constructor, and a builder-written verifier remains self-referential however
strong that constructor becomes.
Figure E1a isolates the matched validation-evidence control. The source-trace recheck passes 176
evaluation executions: 175 P and one F; its 24 rejections comprise 19 F and five P. The self-validation
recheck passes 160: 138 P and 22 F. These joint labels measure recheck precision. They do not
establish why an independently passing execution was rejected or where a failing condition diverged;
those diagnoses require the paired execution packets. Construction-time validation covers all 1,570
seed workflows (Table E4); evaluation rechecks use the separate 200-task population.
Per-application direction of the contrasts.
On the 200-task sample, Trace2World has 180 passes
and 20 confirmed failures; every verdict is P or F, so workflow fidelity is a complete count rather
than a bound. The ten-app set is the complete evaluation population, so paired contrasts are reported
per application rather than as resampled intervals: with ten applications as the unit of replication,
Figure E1b shows all ten paired app differences and marks the macro-app mean; positive values
favor Trace2World. Trace2World exceeds the trace-conditioned and no-trace baselines in all ten
apps. Against GUI-GENESIS-style (a 31.3-pp macro-app lead) and self-validation (12.0 pp), it is
higher in nine apps and lower in one (Taopiaopiao, −5.9 pp in both contrasts). The only inferential
statement is design-based. Under the sharp null that the construction contract changes no application’s
workflow fidelity, the sign of each paired difference is exchangeable; a sign-flip permutation test
enumerates all 210 = 1,024 sign assignments and counts those whose mean is at least as extreme
as the observed mean, which gives exact p = 4/1,024 = 0.004 for the GUI-GENESIS-style
contrast and 6/1,024 = 0.006 for self-validation, while the all-positive contrasts sit at the floor of
2/1,024 = 0.002. The test assumes no population of applications beyond the ten evaluated, and no
interval is estimated.
A final value can conceal a wrong transition.
The proportion of all task packets whose value-only
check passes while their Effect judgment is a confirmed failure is 14.3% for PhoneWorld-style, 10.9%
for CUA-Gym + traces, 15.6% for Direct + traces, 13.2% for GUI-GENESIS-style, and 4.5% for
Trace2World. Figure 2 illustrates the missing-confirmation form of this error.

Protocols.
Each external column is produced under its benchmark’s own protocol: task set, agent
template, observation, step budget, trial count, decoding temperature, and success criterion. Our
policies enter every suite through the suite’s screenshot-only observation and its released action
adapter, with no Trace2World environment, evaluator, or step budget carried over. MobileGym-Bench
resets before each task and grants 15/30/45/60 steps by level (+15 for AnswerSheet tasks); general
models use the generic v2 template and specialized GUI agents their native adapters; SR averages

standard 116-task suite on the reference emulator with its programmatic checkers, a single run
per task, and the suite’s per-task step budget, which the maintainers set at about twice the human
completion length. MobileWorld evaluates its GUI-only track of 117 tasks with a 50-round limit per

E.4.1
BALANCED 100-TASK REAL-DEVICE PROTOCOL AND MATCHED BASELINES
We freeze a paired 100-task panel on 20 policy-training apps, five per app: 50 entity rebindings retain
a seed workflow schema but use unseen values and starts, while 50 workflow identities come from
Trace2World-Bench’s frozen held-out-workflow slice (30 seed-family and 20 augmentation-family).
Ten apps use a 3:2 entity/workflow mix and ten use 2:3, counterbalanced by domain and construction-
trace budget. The workflow half overlaps the benchmark and is not a second independent evaluation
set.
Within each app–family stratum, candidate order and reversible, equivalently restorable eligibility
are frozen before simulator or device outcomes; an ineligible candidate advances within the same
stratum. The same identities are evaluated for three training seeds in both substrates, with substrate
order randomized and goal, start, terminal criterion, and each task’s 30- or 45-step single-application
budget from Table D6 held fixed, so a simulator/device mismatch cannot come from different step
limits. Only live entity values may be rebound after content drift. No panel identity or outcome enters
construction, training, repair, or checkpoint selection.
Agreement is the share of identical simulator/device outcomes, with both directional mismatches
also reported. The real/sim gain ratio is R = [SRreal(π3) −SRreal(π0)]/[SRenv(π3) −SRenv(π0)].

E.5
SCOPE, LIMITATIONS, AND FUTURE WORK
This paper evaluates Trace2World on mobile applications reconstructed as DOM-backed browser
environments from traces collected at one point in time, with proprietary models in the construction,
validation, screening, and review roles. Three limitations follow from this scope, and each points to a
direct extension. First, a browser reimplementation renders differently from the native application,
so the paired device study bounds rather than removes the substrate gap; that panel covers policy-
training applications, while unseen-application transfer is measured in reconstructed environments.
47


Preprint
Extending the paired panel to held-out applications, and the substrate to desktop and native rendering,
are extensions of the same protocol. Second, we report initial construction cost but not refresh
cost. Because every supported relation keeps its source links, application behavior that changes
after collection—a new entry surface, a changed action effect—can be reconciled from new traces
and re-accepted by the same replay, so the intended maintenance path is incremental refresh rather
than rebuild; its accounting is future work. Third, all optimization tasks are single-app, and the
construction roles run on proprietary models with the bounded API stage on the weaker of the two
(Appendix D.2). Since gains already reach the evaluation-only two-app slice, training on validated
cross-app compositions is a natural next step, as are open-weight substitution in each role and,
eventually, using a trained policy’s own validated executions as additional construction evidence to
close the loop from policy back to world.

preserving the shared world, the ordered state changes, and an evaluator-owned pre-commit terminal.

reproduces this single workflow exactly, so agentic construction can realize a task-local workflow.
The distinction appears when several traces must define one reusable application, where identity,
state, and effects have to stay consistent across workflows; Trace2World fixes those semantics before
its own implementation loop begins. These are qualitative mechanism diagnoses, not estimates for
the full construction population.
